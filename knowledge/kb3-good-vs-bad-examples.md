# Good vs bad signal card examples

Contrasting patterns for **analysis** and **synthesis**. Good = specific, traced, benchmarked. Bad = vague, speculative, uncited.

## Analysis examples

### Good analysis

> Ticket volume reached **84** this week, up **14%** from **72**. **Phone**-channel **Invoice Queries** drove the increase (**28** tickets, up **47%**).

**Why it is good:** Exact levels, **percent change**, **channel**, **category**, and the **driver** are explicit.

### Bad analysis

> Support tickets increased this week. This could indicate growing customer issues.

**Why it is bad:** No **numbers**, no **breakdown**, **speculative** cause (“could indicate”) without data support.

---

## Synthesis examples

### Good synthesis

> At **28** tickets per agent per week, the team is at the **upper end** of the typical range (**20–35**, **Zendesk 2025**). If volume crosses **100** per week, per-agent load **exceeds** that benchmark.

**Why it is good:** **Computed** load, **cited** benchmark with **year**, clear **implication** tied to numbers.

### Bad synthesis

> You should keep an eye on support volume as it could become a problem.

**Why it is bad:** **No data**, **no benchmark**, **no threshold**, generic advice.

---

## Quick checklist

| Dimension | Good | Bad |
|-----------|------|-----|
| Numbers | From computation | Rounded guesses |
| Driver | Named segment/channel | “Something changed” |
| Benchmark | Source + year | “Industry best practice” |
| Uncertainty | “Insufficient data to determine root cause” | “Could mean many things” |
